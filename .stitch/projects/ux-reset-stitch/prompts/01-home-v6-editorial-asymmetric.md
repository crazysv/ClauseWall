[An editorial, human, asymmetrical homepage for a contract-safety product that helps ordinary people understand what they are signing. It should feel designed, grounded, and memorable without looking like an AI startup, a law-firm website, or a generic SaaS landing page.]

**CORE DIRECTION:**
- Move away from uniform card grids and repetitive boxed sections.
- Use an editorial composition with uneven layout rhythm, varied block sizes, strong typography, and annotated document visuals.
- The page should feel like a carefully art-directed product experience, not a template.

**HARD CONSTRAINTS:**
- Do not make this look like a generic AI website.
- Do not use the standard “hero + three equal cards + dashboard preview + CTA strip” structure.
- Do not use too many same-sized cards.
- Do not rely on floating glass panels, glowing gradients, neon accents, abstract tech blobs, or fake AI dashboards.
- Do not make the page look cold, hacker-ish, or over-corporate.

**DESIGN TARGET:**
The page should feel like a blend of:
- editorial product storytelling
- public-interest consumer technology
- annotated document design
- a trustworthy financial-safety product

It should feel:
- human
- distinctive
- clear
- composed
- calm
- premium without being luxury-coded

**DESIGN SYSTEM (REQUIRED):**
- Platform: Web, responsive, desktop-first
- Palette: warm paper (#f5f1e8), soft panel cream (#ece5d8), deep ink (#1e2430), muted navy (#465d7a), forest green (#58745f), rust amber (#b67b36), soft brick (#b86464)
- Typography: use a strong editorial serif or display face for the main headline and selected pull-quote moments; pair with a clean readable sans-serif for body and UI
- Components: low-radius or squared edges, subtle separators, thin borders, minimal shadows, textured or tonal depth instead of obvious card stacks

**COMPOSITION RULES:**
- Build the page around 1 or 2 strong visual anchors, not many equal modules.
- Mix text-led sections with document-led sections.
- Use uneven column widths and varied spacing rhythm.
- Let some content breathe instead of boxing everything.
- If cards are used, vary their scale and density so the page feels designed rather than generated.
- Use annotated contract fragments, margin notes, highlights, and excerpts as the main visual language.

**PAGE STRUCTURE:**
1. **Header**
   - restrained navigation
   - logo or wordmark on the left
   - one clear primary CTA on the right

2. **Hero**
   - asymmetric split composition
   - left side: strong plain-English headline and supporting text
   - right side: a believable contract page with highlighted passages, handwritten-style margin notes, or editorial annotation markers
   - one primary CTA and one secondary CTA
   - no giant dashboard or generic product mockup

3. **What ClauseWall Helps You Do**
   - not three matching cards
   - instead, one strong lead statement and 2-3 varied supporting blocks
   - examples:
     - understand the risky parts
     - know what to question
     - keep track if terms change later

4. **How It Works**
   - a simple instructional section
   - three steps maximum
   - visually distinct from the rest of the page
   - more like a guided process strip or annotated sequence than a row of cards

5. **Example Result**
   - one composed, believable analysis preview
   - show:
     - a plain-language summary
     - one highlighted risky clause
     - one practical next step
   - make it feel real and understandable, not feature-dense

6. **Closing Section**
   - calm trust statement
   - final CTA
   - keep it simple and grounded

**CONTENT TONE:**
- plain English
- reassuring
- practical
- respectful
- non-hype

Avoid phrases like:
- AI-powered
- legal intelligence
- next-generation
- transform workflows
- revolutionize compliance

Prefer phrases like:
- understand what you’re agreeing to
- spot the parts that matter
- get clear next steps
- make a more informed decision

**MOTION:**
- subtle only
- small editorial reveals
- slight annotation or highlight motion in the contract visual
- restrained hover feedback
- no dramatic scroll scenes or cinematic choreography

**EXTRA INSTRUCTION:**
Make the page feel intentionally art-directed and structurally varied. It should not feel like repeated components placed in a neat grid. It should feel like a real product homepage with a distinctive point of view.
